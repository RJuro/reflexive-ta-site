Introduction · ~3 min spoken rendering reads the prose; the anchors stay on screen

Mary Grande left a three-room peasant house near Novo Mesto in 1920, aged ten — a household her mother ran alone, farming and bartering cattle, while her father had been in the United States since 1911. DP·S1.014 The journey itself runs on people, not institutions: when the family’s clothes are stolen in Trieste, it is “a fellow that my mother knew” who buys replacements in London. DP·S1.017

What she dwells on longest is not hardship but texture — the barn under the same roof, a pet cow she “loved very much,” animals as ordinary company. DP·S2.031 That warmth sits oddly against the economic frame this project has been building, and I have not resolved it.

For the story: this interview mostly confirms what Rodwin gave us about households absorbing migration’s costs — and complicates the word “kin.”
